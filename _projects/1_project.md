My graduate work regarded the development of statistical tests for detecting structural breaks in time series data, which has implications to a wide variety of fields, including climatology and economics. I first illustrated adjustments that should be made to basic mean shift tests to account for autocorrelation {% cite jtsa11 %} and then developed procedures that were applied to detect shifts in the historical record of tropical cyclones in the Atlantic {% cite jasa11 %} (showing a recent increase in the number of storms but not in their strength).<br>

I established a research agenda that involved modification of these methods for an increasingly wide range of data {% cite environ12 joc13 ecci20 %}, culminating in the development of novel procedures for application in data that are subject to an underlying non-stationary regression-based trend in addition to autocorrelated errors and seasonality {% cite jasa16 statsin20 %}, which was applied to detect shifts in the quadratic seasonal trend in CO2 levels measured at Mauna Loa, Hawaii. Throughout this work, I developed an expertise in time series analysis, which led to further research output {% cite statsin18 amstat19 %}, including the design of procedures for assessing the presence of cross correlation in multivariate time series {% cite jbes15 %} with applications to financial data.
